# Health

Health is shaped by more than healthcare. It is influenced by the places people live, the air they breathe, the water they drink, the information they receive and the systems that support them.

My work in this sector focuses on making health information clearer and more usable. I translate public-health, clinical and data-led material into communication that supports understanding, engagement and better decisions.

## Why it matters

Health is connected to environment, climate, education and technology. Air pollution, extreme heat, flooding, unequal access to services and misinformation all affect health outcomes.

Public health depends not only on treatment, but also on prevention, early awareness, reliable information and the ability of people and services to respond effectively. Clear communication is therefore part of the system, not an optional extra.

## My perspective

I approach health as a system involving people, evidence, services and context.

A health message is most useful when it is accurate, accessible and relevant to the person receiving it. That means explaining what is known, what is uncertain and what practical action is appropriate—without creating unnecessary fear or false reassurance.

My background is in physics, and my work sits at the intersection of health, environment, climate and communication. I am particularly interested in how data, digital tools and clear narratives can support public understanding and more effective services.

## The work I do

### Health communication

I help explain health topics in ways that are accurate, accessible and appropriate for their audience.

This can include public-health messaging, service information, campaign content, data explanations and materials for clinical, community or policy audiences.

### Making health data meaningful

Health data can reveal patterns in disease, service use, risk, access and outcomes. But data alone does not create understanding.

I help turn data into clear narratives and visual explanations, connecting evidence to real-world questions while respecting privacy, uncertainty and context.

### Climate, environment and health

Environmental conditions affect health. Air quality, heat, flooding, water quality and climate-related hazards can influence both immediate health risks and longer-term wellbeing.

I am interested in communication that makes these connections clear, helping people and organisations understand risk and prepare effectively.

### Digital tools and information

Digital tools can support health communication when they make reliable information easier to find, understand and act on.

My focus is on clarity, accessibility and appropriate use of evidence. A useful health tool should help people make informed decisions, not replace professional advice or oversimplify complex issues.
