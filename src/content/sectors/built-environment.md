# Built environment

The built environment is where climate, technology, design and everyday life meet. Buildings, streets, infrastructure and public spaces shape how people move, use energy, experience their surroundings and respond to environmental change.

My work in this sector focuses on making the built environment easier to understand and improve: communicating the role of buildings and infrastructure in sustainability, resilience and low-carbon transition, and helping make complex technical information accessible to the people who need it.

## Why it matters

The built environment has a major influence on emissions, resource use, health, accessibility and resilience. Buildings consume energy, embody carbon in their materials, shape transport patterns and affect how communities experience heat, flooding and air pollution.

A sustainable built environment is therefore not only about new construction. It also involves retrofitting existing buildings, improving infrastructure, planning places that reduce car dependency, and designing public spaces that support wellbeing and climate resilience.

## My perspective

I approach the built environment as a system of decisions: what is built, where, how it is operated, who it serves, and how it changes over time.

My interest is in making those decisions more visible and understandable. That includes explaining the relationship between buildings and energy, infrastructure and climate risk, urban design and wellbeing, and heritage and sustainable change.

I am particularly interested in how clear communication and digital tools can support better understanding of places, from local environmental impacts to the practical choices involved in retrofit, planning and resilience.

## The work I do

### Communicating places and systems

I help explain how buildings, infrastructure and urban systems work, and why their design and operation matter.

This can involve explaining energy use, retrofit approaches, planning decisions, transport connections, environmental risks or the relationship between a place and its wider context.

### Climate resilience and adaptation

The built environment needs to withstand changing conditions, including heat, flooding, drought and severe weather. Adaptation involves understanding risk, designing for resilience and making long-term decisions that do not lock in future problems.

I help communicate these issues in ways that make local relevance clear, without reducing complex risks to simplistic warnings.

### Low-carbon transition

Decarbonising the built environment involves operational energy, embodied carbon, retrofit, heating, transport and the materials used in construction.

I am interested in how organisations, public bodies and communities can communicate transition plans and progress honestly, including trade-offs, uncertainty and the difference between ambition and delivery.

### Digital tools and public understanding

Digital tools can make places and systems easier to explore. Catalogues, maps, visual narratives and accessible data-led resources can help people understand what exists, what is changing and what options are available.

My aim is to create useful entry points rather than duplicate detailed technical documentation.
