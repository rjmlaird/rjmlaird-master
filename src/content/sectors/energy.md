# Energy

Energy systems underpin modern life. They power homes, industry, transport and digital infrastructure, and they are central to the transition away from fossil fuels.

My work in this sector focuses on making energy systems and decarbonisation easier to understand. I translate technical, policy and data-led material into clear communication that explains what is changing, why it matters and what practical choices are involved.

## Why it matters

Decarbonising energy is one of the central tasks of climate mitigation. It involves changing how electricity is generated, how buildings are heated, how transport is powered and how industry uses energy.

This is not only a technology challenge. It also involves infrastructure, markets, regulation, planning, public acceptance and the practical realities of maintaining reliable services during transition.

A credible energy transition needs to explain both the opportunities and the constraints: renewable generation, grid flexibility, storage, electrification, efficiency and the continuing need for reliable, affordable energy.

## My perspective

I approach energy as a system, not a collection of isolated technologies.

Solar panels, wind turbines, batteries, heat pumps, grids and electric vehicles are connected. Their value depends on how they work together, how demand changes over time, and how the system remains reliable as it decarbonises.

My background is in physics, and my work sits at the intersection of energy, climate, sustainability and communication. I am particularly interested in making technical systems understandable without oversimplifying the engineering, economics or policy context.

## The work I do

### Explaining energy systems

I help explain how energy is generated, moved, stored and used.

This can include renewable generation, electricity grids, storage, heat, transport electrification, industrial energy use and the interactions between them.

The aim is to make the system legible: showing not only individual technologies, but how they fit together and what they enable.

### Communicating decarbonisation

Decarbonisation involves difficult choices and long timescales. Clear communication needs to distinguish between ambition, policy, deployment and delivered outcomes.

I help organisations and projects explain their role in the transition, including what they are doing, why it matters, what evidence supports it and what limitations remain.

### Making data meaningful

Energy data can reveal patterns in generation, demand, emissions and system performance. But raw data alone rarely changes understanding.

I help turn data into accessible narratives and visual explanations, connecting numbers to real-world questions such as reliability, cost, emissions, resilience and local impact.

### Supporting public understanding

Energy transition affects people directly: through bills, planning decisions, local infrastructure, transport and the reliability of everyday services.

Clear communication can help people engage with those decisions more confidently, understanding both the need for change and the practical questions involved in delivering it.
