# Space

Space is where scientific curiosity meets practical value on Earth. Satellites observe weather, oceans, land, ice and the atmosphere; missions test new technologies; and space-derived data increasingly informs climate action, emergency response, policy and public understanding.

My work in this sector focuses on making that value visible and usable. I translate complex missions, Earth-observation data and technical research into clear stories, communication assets and digital tools—without losing the scientific accuracy or uncertainty behind them.

## Why space matters

Space is often imagined as distant and abstract, but much of its most immediate value comes from looking back at Earth.

Satellite observations provide a consistent, global view that is difficult to obtain from the ground. They can monitor weather systems, oceans, forests, ice, air quality and atmospheric composition across regions where ground-based measurements are sparse or unavailable.

For example, ESA’s Aeolus mission used a Doppler wind lidar to provide global profiles of wind from the surface up to around 30 km, supporting weather forecasting and research into atmospheric dynamics.

Europe’s Copernicus Sentinel missions similarly observe land, ocean, atmosphere and emergency-response needs. Sentinel-1 provides radar observations of land, sea ice, oceans and marine environments; Sentinel-3 measures sea and land surface temperature, ocean colour and sea-surface topography; and atmospheric missions monitor gases and aerosols relevant to air quality and climate.

The challenge is rarely whether the data exists. It is helping people understand what it means, what it can and cannot tell them, and how it should inform decisions.

## My perspective

I approach space as a communication and translation problem as much as a technology problem.

A mission may involve sophisticated instrumentation, orbital mechanics, materials science, data processing and international collaboration. But its public value depends on whether different audiences can understand why it matters: scientists, policymakers, journalists, students, partners and the wider public.

My background is in physics, and my work sits at the intersection of space, sustainability and science communication. I am particularly interested in Earth observation: how satellite data can make environmental and climate processes more tangible, and how clear communication can turn technical capability into public understanding and practical action.

## The work I do

My space work generally falls into four connected areas.

### Mission and Earth-observation communication

I develop communication strategies, narratives and content that explain missions, instruments and data in ways that are accurate and engaging.

This can include campaign planning, video concepts, editorial content, visual storytelling, social-media assets and materials for technical and non-technical audiences.

The aim is not simply to describe what a mission does. It is to show why it exists, what problem it addresses, how its data is used, and what difference it can make.

### Making satellite data meaningful

Satellite data becomes most useful when it is connected to a question people care about: How is the climate changing? Where is flooding likely? What is happening to air quality? How can communities prepare for environmental risk?

I help shape narratives and digital experiences that connect observations to context. That includes explaining what a measurement represents, where its limitations lie, and why uncertainty is part of good science rather than a weakness.

### Science communication and public engagement

Space offers a powerful way into physics, geography, data literacy and environmental awareness. I create and support communication that makes complex subjects accessible without reducing them to slogans or oversimplified claims.

This includes explaining missions and instruments, framing the relevance of satellite observations, and helping audiences distinguish between what is known, what is uncertain and what remains an open question.

### Digital tools and knowledge sharing

Alongside communication work, I build digital tools and resources that make information easier to explore. These may include catalogues, data-led visualisations, educational resources or lightweight APIs that make structured information more reusable.

The goal is not to duplicate documentation. It is to provide useful entry points: helping people discover relevant work, understand its context and move on to the authoritative source.

## Areas of focus

### Earth observation

Earth observation is central to my interest in space. It connects orbital technology directly to environmental and societal questions.

Satellite systems can observe changes across land, ocean, ice and atmosphere. Copernicus Sentinel data, for example, supports applications ranging from land management and agriculture to marine monitoring, emergency response and climate services.

I am particularly interested in how these observations can support climate adaptation, environmental monitoring and place-based storytelling.

### Climate and sustainability

Space data is increasingly important for understanding and responding to climate change. Observations of the atmosphere, oceans, ice, land surface and extreme events can support monitoring, modelling, preparedness and communication.

The connection between space and climate is not only technical. Effective climate communication needs to make evidence meaningful at a human scale: showing what changing data means for particular places, communities and decisions.

### Mission communication

Space missions involve long development cycles, specialist teams and high levels of technical complexity. Communication has to make that work intelligible without turning it into marketing language.

I focus on clear narratives, credible explanations and assets that can be reused across channels. This includes helping audiences understand not only what a mission achieves, but also the research, testing and collaboration behind it.

### Space policy and the space economy

The space sector is also shaped by governance, international cooperation, regulation, sustainability and commercial development. These issues affect how space is used, who benefits from it, and how its risks are managed.

I am interested in making these discussions more accessible, particularly where they connect to Earth observation, climate services, responsible use of space and public value.

## How I work

My approach combines scientific literacy, editorial judgement and practical delivery.

I start by understanding the audience and the decision they need to make. Then I identify the core idea, the evidence behind it and the most useful way to communicate it. Finally, I turn that into formats people can actually use: articles, videos, visual narratives, websites, catalogues or digital tools.

I aim to be precise about uncertainty, careful with claims and clear about sources. Good space communication should make complexity easier to engage with—not make it disappear.
