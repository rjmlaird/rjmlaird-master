# Fisheries

Fisheries connect marine ecosystems, food systems, coastal communities and economic activity. They involve the management of wild fish stocks, the farming of aquatic organisms, seafood supply chains and the policies that shape how these systems are used.

My work in this sector focuses on making fisheries science, management and sustainability easier to understand. I translate technical and policy material into clear communication that respects both ecological complexity and the people whose livelihoods depend on it.

## Why it matters

Fisheries are part of wider marine ecosystems. Their long-term viability depends on healthy stocks, functioning habitats, effective management and an understanding of the pressures facing ocean systems.

Sustainable fisheries aim to maintain biological and economic productivity over the long term without causing ecological changes that reduce options for future generations. 

Aquaculture is the farming of aquatic organisms, including fish, molluscs, crustaceans and aquatic plants. It is distinct from capture fisheries, although the two are connected through seafood systems, markets and marine resource management. 

## My perspective

I approach fisheries as a system involving ecology, evidence, livelihoods and governance.

Fish stocks are not isolated resources. They are part of ecosystems affected by fishing pressure, habitat condition, pollution, climate change and management decisions. Effective fisheries management brings together information, analysis, consultation, regulation and implementation to support the long-term sustainable use of fisheries resources. 

My interest is in making these relationships clear: showing how science informs management, how management affects ecosystems and communities, and why uncertainty needs to be handled honestly.

## The work I do

### Fisheries and marine communication

I help explain fisheries science, management and sustainability in ways that are accessible without being simplistic.

This can include explaining stock assessment, quotas, fishing pressure, aquaculture, seafood traceability or the relationship between fisheries and wider ocean health.

### Making data meaningful

Fisheries rely on data: catch records, survey data, stock assessments, environmental observations and management indicators.

I help turn this information into clear narratives and visual explanations, showing what the data can tell us, what it cannot, and why uncertainty matters.

### Communicating sustainability

Sustainability in fisheries involves ecological, economic and social considerations. It is not only about the quantity of fish caught; it also concerns ecosystem health, community livelihoods, food supply and long-term resilience.

I help communicate these connections without reducing complex issues to simple labels.

### Supporting informed decisions

Fisheries management affects fishers, coastal communities, regulators, scientists and consumers. Clear communication can help these audiences understand the evidence, trade-offs and practical implications of different approaches.
