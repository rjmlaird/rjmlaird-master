# Space for Neurodiversity

Helping neurodivergent people explore, enter and find their place in the space sector.

## Overview

Space for Neurodiversity is an initiative I am developing to help neurodivergent people discover opportunities in the space sector and take meaningful steps towards education, experience and employment.

The ambition is to connect an interest in space with clearer, more accessible pathways into it: helping people understand the possibilities, develop relevant skills and confidence, and find opportunities that work for them.

Alongside this, I want to encourage a sector where inclusion shapes how people are welcomed, supported and able to contribute.

## Why I am developing it

My motivation brings together my interests in space, science communication and widening participation.

I want to help make the journey from “I’m interested in space” to “I can see a place for myself here” more understandable and achievable. That means looking beyond awareness alone and considering the practical steps between curiosity, learning and participation.

The intention is not to suggest that all neurodivergent people have the same needs, strengths or ambitions. It is to build an initiative that makes room for individual differences and develops with the people it aims to support.

## What I am working towards

The initiative’s developing focus includes:

- Exploring opportunities: making the range of space-sector roles and routes into them easier to understand.
- Supporting learning: developing accessible educational content and opportunities to build relevant skills.
- Connecting people: creating opportunities for neurodivergent people to meet peers, educators and space-sector professionals.
- Supporting progression: helping people identify practical next steps towards further study, experience or employment.
- Encouraging inclusion: engaging with organisations about how they can make participation more accessible and supportive.

These are areas of development, rather than a claim that a full programme of services is already available.

## My role

I am shaping the initiative’s purpose, strategy and public-facing communications, bringing together my background in astrophysics, science communication, marketing and digital development.

My work includes developing its identity and website, refining who it is for and what it should offer, and planning how an initial idea can become a focused, sustainable initiative.

A central priority is turning a broad ambition into something practical: a clear audience, an achievable first offer and a way to learn from participants’ experiences.

## Current stage

Space for Neurodiversity remains an ongoing initiative in development.

The immediate focus is on refining its offer, building relationships and establishing the foundations for activity that can be delivered responsibly and evaluated honestly.

I am also exploring a future Community Interest Company structure. This is a development goal, not a statement that the initiative is already incorporated.

## Longer-term ambition

I want Space for Neurodiversity to help make participation in the space sector more accessible, with education, community and practical progression at its heart.

Success would mean more than raising awareness. It would mean helping people take meaningful next steps, learning from what supports them, and using that experience to improve the initiative over time.
