## Overview

This project investigated unidentified absorption features in the far-ultraviolet
(FUV) spectra of hydrogen-deficient white dwarfs, particularly DO white dwarfs
and PG1159 stars. The work used spectra obtained by the **Far Ultraviolet
Spectroscopic Explorer (FUSE)**, a space telescope that observed in the
far-ultraviolet region.

The central problem was that many absorption lines in these stellar spectra had
not been securely identified. Some features could originate in the star’s
photosphere; others could be produced by material in the interstellar medium
(ISM) between the star and Earth. Distinguishing between these origins is an
essential first step before assigning an atomic species or transition to an
unidentified line.

## Research question

How can radial-velocity differences between white dwarfs and the interstellar
medium be used to determine whether unidentified FUV absorption lines are
photospheric or interstellar in origin?

A related question was whether the remaining unidentified features could be
explained by incomplete atomic line lists, including transitions from highly
ionised or iron-group species not adequately represented in available data.

## Method

The analysis used velocity discrimination. In a simplified case, spectra from
two white dwarfs have different separations between their photospheric and ISM
radial velocities.

- A line that shifts with the ISM velocity is likely interstellar.
- A line that shifts with the photospheric velocity is likely stellar.
- Comparing the same feature across stars helps distinguish the two possible
  origins even where a line’s atomic identification is initially unknown.
