## Overview

**BreezoMeter** is a global environmental-information service that provides
local, personalised information about air quality and pollen.

The service combines big-data methods and machine learning with multiple
environmental data sources, including government monitoring stations, traffic
information, meteorological conditions and data from the Copernicus Atmosphere
Monitoring Service (**CAMS**).

This project formed part of the **European State of the Climate 2017** showcase
work for ECMWF and Copernicus. I contributed editorial and
science-communication work through Design & Data, helping explain how
large-scale atmospheric data can become clear, useful information for
individuals and organisations.

## The challenge

Air pollution and pollen are often invisible, variable and highly local.

People may know that poor air quality or high pollen levels can affect health,
comfort and daily routines, but they do not necessarily have the time or
technical knowledge to interpret pollutant concentrations, monitoring networks,
weather models or forecast uncertainty.

The communications challenge was to explain a sophisticated data pipeline in
terms of everyday questions:

- What is the air quality where I am?
- Which pollutant is likely to matter most today?
- Are pollen conditions likely to affect allergies?
- When is a better time to exercise, commute or spend time outdoors?
- How can a service provide useful information where monitoring stations are
  sparse or absent?

BreezoMeter’s public value came from making these questions easier to answer.

## The service

BreezoMeter’s aim was to provide air-quality and pollen information in a form
that users could understand as readily as a weather forecast.

The service reached both businesses and consumers through:

- A mobile application.
- An interactive website.
- Application programming interfaces (APIs).
- Data products for external platforms and organisations.

Users could explore current air-pollution and pollen conditions for a selected
location, alongside forecast information and personalised recommendations.

## Data approach

BreezoMeter combined several data layers to estimate local environmental
conditions:

- Government monitoring stations
- Satellite observations
- Traffic information
- Weather and meteorological conditions
- CAMS atmospheric data
- Machine learning and big-data analytics

Together, these sources produce local air-quality and pollen information.

## My role

I worked as an **Editor and science communicator** through Design & Data.

My contribution included:

- Developing editorial communication for an ECMWF and CAMS use case.
- Explaining machine-learning-enabled environmental information in accessible language.
- Connecting atmospheric data with health, wellbeing and everyday decisions.
- Supporting video and multimedia storytelling for the ESOTC 2017 showcase.

## Outcomes

The work produced a public-facing explanation of a global downstream Copernicus service, presented as part of the European State of the Climate 2017 showcase and supported by a short video.

## Limitations

This project concerned editorial communication. It did not involve developing BreezoMeter's forecasting models, operating CAMS or producing the underlying air-quality data.

Air-quality and pollen forecasts are subject to uncertainty and local variation, and public communication should explain both their usefulness and their limits.
