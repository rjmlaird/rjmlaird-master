## Overview

**Windy** was a communications project connected to the European Centre for
Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF), the Copernicus programme and the use of
interactive visualisation to make environmental data easier to understand.

The work focused on explaining how complex forecast and monitoring data can be
made useful to non-specialist audiences through familiar, map-based interfaces.
Windy presents changing weather and environmental conditions as an interactive
global visualisation, allowing users to explore variables such as wind, rain,
cloud, temperature and air quality.

I contributed editorial and science-communication work through Design & Data,
supporting clear public storytelling around atmospheric data, Copernicus
services and the practical value of environmental information.

## Context

Weather and atmospheric information is technically complex. Forecasting systems
combine physics-based models with observations from satellites, weather
stations, aircraft, ships, balloons and other instruments.

For public audiences, the usefulness of this information depends not only on
scientific quality but on whether it can be understood and applied. Interactive
maps offer a practical way to show changing conditions across time and place.

Windy is a weather-visualisation service that displays a range of forecast
layers over an interactive global map. It uses ECMWF weather information
alongside other sources to present variables including wind, rain, lightning
and cloud types. [web:219][web:226]

## The challenge

The communications challenge was to connect three different levels of the
story:

- **Scientific infrastructure:** ECMWF forecasting and Copernicus monitoring
  systems.
- **Environmental information:** weather, atmospheric composition, air quality
  and aerosol forecasts.
- **Public value:** a visual interface through which people can explore
  conditions relevant to daily life, travel, safety and environmental awareness.

This required translating terms such as atmospheric composition, particulate
matter, nitrogen dioxide, numerical modelling and forecast layers into clear,
practical language without overstating certainty or simplifying away the
underlying science.

## CAMS and ECMWF

The Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (**CAMS**) provides information
about atmospheric composition, including air pollutants, aerosols and other
components that affect air quality, climate and human health.

ECMWF implements CAMS on behalf of the European Union as part of the Copernicus
Earth-observation programme. [web:227][web:221]

CAMS combines atmospheric models with satellite and in-situ observations to
produce daily analyses and forecasts of atmospheric composition. Its data can
support work on air quality, dust transport, wildfire smoke, volcanic ash,
solar energy and climate-related environmental monitoring. [web:226][web:228]

## Windy partnership

In 2019, CAMS partnered with Windy to bring global air-quality information to
the Windy web and smartphone platform.

The integration included forecasts for:

- Nitrogen dioxide.
- Fine particulate matter.
- Wind-blown dust.
- Sea salt aerosol.
- Volcanic ash.
- Biomass-burning pollution and smoke.

CAMS supplied daily forecasts, while Windy made the information accessible
through its visual map interface. Copernicus reported that Windy served around
800,000 users per day at the time the partnership was announced. [web:219]

## My role

I worked as an **Editor and science communicator** through Design & Data.

My contribution supported editorial development and public-facing communication
around ECMWF, Copernicus and environmental-data visualisation. The work focused
on presenting atmospheric science in a way that retained technical credibility
while remaining useful to audiences outside specialist forecasting,
meteorological and Earth-observation communities.

This included helping shape narratives around:

- The relationship between weather forecasts and atmospheric monitoring.
- The role of ECMWF in producing and providing environmental information.
- The value of Copernicus services for public understanding and decision-making.
- How interactive visualisation can make large environmental datasets more
  approachable.
- The practical relevance of air-quality forecasts and atmospheric monitoring.

## Editorial approach

The communication approach was based on a simple principle:

> Environmental data becomes more useful when people can see where it applies,
> understand what it means and explore how it changes over time.

The project used map-based visualisation as the bridge between complex
forecasting infrastructure and everyday interpretation.

This approach helped frame atmospheric data not as an abstract technical
resource, but as information that can support decisions about health, travel,
outdoor activity, aviation, emergency response and environmental awareness.

## Outcomes

The work contributed to communications around the use of Copernicus and ECMWF
data in public-facing weather and environmental visualisation.

Key outcomes included:

- Clearer storytelling around atmospheric forecast data.
- Public-facing explanation of CAMS air-quality information.
- Support for communications linking ECMWF, Copernicus and Windy.
- Editorial framing of interactive environmental-data visualisation.
- Video and multimedia material associated with the project.

## Limitations

This project concerned editorial communication and public understanding of
environmental information. It did not involve producing ECMWF forecast models,
operating Copernicus services or developing the Windy platform itself.

Forecast and monitoring products remain subject to uncertainty, spatial
resolution limits, data availability and the interpretation constraints
associated with any environmental model. Public communication should therefore
help audiences understand both the value and the limits of forecast
information.
