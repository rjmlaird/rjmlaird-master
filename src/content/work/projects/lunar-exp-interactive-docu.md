## Overview

**Lunar Exploration Interactive Documentary** is an ESA multimedia web
experience that helps audiences explore the Moon through science, technology,
missions and expert voices.

The documentary includes more than 40 videos featuring scientists involved in
lunar research. Visitors can follow their own route through subjects including
the Moon’s history, human exploration, robotic missions, lunar resources,
spacecraft technologies and future exploration plans. 

My role focused on **editorial development and content promotion**: shaping
complex source material into accessible public stories, planning thematic
campaign content and connecting audiences from ESA social channels to relevant
areas of the web documentary.

## My editorial role

I worked as an **Editorial Contributor and Content-Promotion Planner** through
Design & Data.

The work was not simply about announcing the website. It involved identifying
the most compelling stories within a large library of interviews, articles,
technologies and mission material, then adapting them for different audiences
and channels.

My responsibilities included:

- Developing clear editorial angles from technical and scientific source
  material.
- Writing and editing short-form copy for social-media posts.
- Preparing longer contextual copy for campaigns and content briefs.
- Selecting documentary content that matched a planned topic, event or
  publishing moment.
- Creating clear pathways from social posts to relevant web-documentary pages.
- Supporting promotion of the web-documentary trailer.
- Planning a recurring publishing calendar from December 2016 to June 2017.
- Aligning content with Full Moons, lunar-science events and exploration news.
- Helping ensure terminology, tone and claims were appropriate for public
  science communication.

## Editorial challenge

The documentary covered a broad and technically demanding field.

Topics ranged from the formation of the Moon and the history of the Solar System
to robotics, life support, sample drilling, navigation, communications,
radiation, spacecraft landing systems and human exploration.

The editorial task was to preserve the meaning of the science while making each
story understandable at a glance.

Specialist interview or technical briefing
→ Identify the human or scientific question
→ Write a concise public-facing narrative
→ Link to the relevant documentary video or section
→ Invite deeper exploration

For example, a story about a lunar drill could be framed not as a description
of hardware specifications, but as a question with immediate relevance:

> How can scientists collect water ice from the Moon without losing the very
> material they want to study?

## Editorial principles

The content strategy followed several principles.

### Start with curiosity

Posts began with a question, challenge or surprising fact rather than an
institutional description.

Examples included:

- Could astronauts grow food and recycle waste on the Moon?
- How can a spacecraft land safely in a cratered polar landscape?
- Why are scientists drilling beneath the lunar surface?
- What can decades-old Moon rocks still tell us today?
- How could astronauts control robots on the Moon from orbit?

### Use expert voices

The documentary was structured around researchers, engineers and mission
specialists. Editorial copy introduced their expertise while keeping the
language direct and readable.

The platform’s science content includes contributors from ESA, DLR, CNRS and
universities, covering subjects such as lunar origins, resources, Solar System
history and human exploration. 

### Make technical content useful

Each story was connected to a reason it matters:

| Topic | Editorial framing |
| --- | --- |
| MELiSSA | Helping astronauts sustain themselves on long missions |
| PILOT | Landing safely in difficult lunar terrain |
| PROSPECT | Drilling for and protecting samples of lunar volatiles |
| METERON | Letting astronauts control robots from orbit |
| Orion European Service Module | Supporting people beyond low Earth orbit |
| Lunar Communications Pathfinder | Returning vital data from hard-to-reach locations |
| Radiation | Reducing risks to future lunar explorers |
| Lunar samples | Using the Moon to investigate the Solar System’s history |

### Offer a next step

Every campaign item was designed to point beyond itself.

A reader could encounter a short social post, watch an expert clip or follow a
link to a particular library item, then discover connected material in the
interactive documentary.

## Content-promotion campaign

I helped develop a promotional programme running from **December 2016 to June
2017**.

The campaign used the site as a living editorial resource rather than a
one-off launch product. It drew on existing documentary assets to create
continuing reasons for audiences to return.

Lunar moment, event or topic
→ Social post or trailer
→ Link to a relevant documentary asset
→ Deeper exploration of related stories

The programme included:

- ESA portal and social-media promotion.
- Trailer publication on YouTube.
- Facebook, Twitter/X and Instagram copy.
- Full-Moon content moments.
- Technology-focused weekly stories.
- Science-focused features tied to expert interviews.
- Content connected to conferences and public-engagement events.
- Promotion through ESA and relevant programme accounts.

## Launch editorial work

The launch activity in December 2016 paired broad visibility with targeted
stories.

A feature on the ESA portal generated approximately 2,000 sessions. Social
amplification through Instagram, ESA accounts, Facebook and a post by ESA
astronaut Thomas Pesquet generated additional referral traffic in the campaign
records.

The editorial lesson was clear: distinct voices and well-chosen entry points
could introduce different audiences to the same in-depth resource.

The campaign then moved from launch messaging to a sustained programme of
topic-led stories.

## Trailer promotion

In February 2017, the campaign supported publication of the documentary
trailer on YouTube.

Editorial work included preparing the destination message, video description,
tags and links that directed viewers to the main site. The trailer was designed
to show that the web documentary was not just a video series, but an
interactive experience that visitors could explore in their own way.

ESA’s trailer description identifies the platform as a web documentary with
more than 40 scientist-led videos and encourages viewers to explore science,
technology and lunar missions through the site. 

## Technology editorial stories

### MELiSSA: life support

One campaign story introduced **MELiSSA** through the challenge of sustaining
astronauts during long-duration missions.

The editorial narrative explained that a lunar base or Mars mission cannot rely
only on resupply from Earth. Future crews need life-support systems that manage
oxygen, carbon dioxide, water, food and waste.

Rather than beginning with programme terminology, the copy led with a simple
human question:

How can astronauts live sustainably when there is no air, water or food outside
their spacecraft?

### PILOT: autonomous landing

A second story focused on **PILOT**, a technology concept for landing
precisely and safely.

The editorial framing explained why polar terrain matters: it may contain water
ice and locations with useful illumination, but it is also rugged and difficult
to reach.

The copy connected navigation, hazard detection and autonomous landing with the
practical challenge of getting a spacecraft safely to scientifically valuable
terrain.

### PROSPECT: collecting lunar samples

The **PROSPECT** story focused on a drill intended to collect and analyse
material beneath the lunar surface.

The editorial challenge was to explain why drilling is difficult in a vacuum at
extreme temperatures, especially when the target material may contain volatile
components that can be altered by heat.

The content connected engineering detail with scientific purpose:

Drill beneath the surface
→ Recover cold, volatile-rich samples
→ Preserve them for analysis
→ Learn more about water and lunar history

ESA’s documentary content describes PROSPECT as a package for collecting and
analysing lunar samples, while later ESA material confirms its focus on drilling
and analysing polar material in the search for volatiles. 

### METERON: humans and robots

A METERON feature explored the possibility of astronauts controlling
semi-autonomous robots from an orbiting space station.

The editorial story used the human-robot partnership as its central idea:
people can provide judgement and direction, while robots can work in difficult,
remote or hazardous locations.

This made telerobotics a concrete future-exploration story rather than an
abstract technology demonstration.

### Orion European Service Module

The editorial angle for the **Orion European Service Module** was that human
exploration depends on fundamental systems that are mostly invisible to
passengers and audiences.

The copy explained that the module provides essential resources and functions,
including power, propulsion, water, gases, thermal management and course
control.

The story framed these systems as the infrastructure that enables human journeys
beyond low Earth orbit.

### Lunar Communications Pathfinder

A separate editorial story focused on communication.

The text compared exploring the Moon with exploring an unfamiliar place through
human senses. Spacecraft use instruments as their remote senses, but the
measurements they collect must be transmitted back to Earth.

This framing helped explain the importance of a communications service for
regions such as the lunar far side and south-polar areas.

## Science editorial stories

### Water in lunar samples

Campaign content drew on expert interviews to explain why old Apollo and Luna
samples can yield new results.

The editorial approach contrasted early analyses with later methods, presenting
the subject as a story of scientific progress: new techniques can reveal
evidence that earlier instruments could not detect.

This created an accessible route into the question of lunar water and why polar
exploration matters.

### Moon formation

Posts tied to lunar-science events introduced competing explanations for the
formation of the Earth-Moon system.

The relevant documentary section includes expert discussion of lunar origins
and evolution, using Moon rocks and planetary research to explore how the Moon
formed. [web:336]

### Solar System history

The Moon’s surface and rocks preserve records that are often erased on Earth by
weather, tectonics and active geology.

Editorial content used this contrast to explain why lunar samples and surface
materials can help scientists investigate impacts, cosmic radiation and the
history of the Solar System.

## Human exploration editorial stories

### Landing systems

A May feature translated the mechanics of spacecraft touchdown into a clear
story about stability.

Landing on a surface filled with slopes, boulders, craters and loose material
requires systems that absorb impact and keep a lander stable enough to operate
instruments, robotic arms or rovers.

The editorial framing moved from a familiar word — *touchdown* — to the
engineering work hidden within it.

### Radiation

A second feature explained that the Moon does not have Earth’s dense atmosphere
or global magnetic field.

The copy introduced the radiation risk facing future explorers and connected
that challenge to the need for sensors, measurement and mitigation strategies.

The aim was to explain the issue without overstating certainty or offering
health guidance.

## Event-based editorial planning

The campaign also identified opportunities to connect the documentary with
events where lunar science and exploration were already under discussion.

### EGU General Assembly

For the 2017 European Geosciences Union General Assembly, the campaign proposed
posts on:

- Lunar samples and meteorites.
- The origin and evolution of the Moon.
- The Moon’s record of Solar System history.
- Questions about early Earth and the origins of life.

The role of the editorial copy was to make the conference connection relevant
to non-attendees: audiences could use the documentary to explore the ideas being
discussed in Vienna.

### European Lunar Symposium

For the European Lunar Symposium, planned stories highlighted:

- Theories of Moon formation.
- In-situ resources for future exploration.
- Radiation records preserved on the lunar surface.
- Interviews with contributing lunar scientists.

### ECSITE and Paris Air Show

The documentary was also prepared for presentation at the ECSITE Conference and
the International Paris Air Show.

For these settings, editorial work supported the framing of the documentary as
both a public-learning resource and a live demonstration of ESA’s lunar
exploration ambitions.

## Editorial outcomes

My contribution helped establish a repeatable content model for a large
interactive documentary.

Instead of treating the site as a single finished product, the campaign treated
it as an editorial library:

- One expert interview could become a social post.
- One technology topic could become a public question.
- One science event could become a reason to rediscover related content.
- One Full Moon could become a timely invitation to explore the site.

This model extended the life of the documentary and made its extensive archive
of expert material easier to discover.

## Limitations

My role was editorial and campaign-focused. I did not lead the overall website
design, software development, scientific research, spacecraft engineering or
ESA mission management.

Campaign traffic figures were recorded at specific moments and should be treated
as indicative referral measures, not a complete attribution model for all
audience behaviour. Mission plans, technologies and schedules presented in the
2016–17 documentary should also be checked against current ESA programme
information.
