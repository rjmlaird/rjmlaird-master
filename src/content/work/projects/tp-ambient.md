## Overview

AMBIEnT (Affordable Microsatellite-Based Internet Access and Environmental
Monitoring) was a conceptual design study completed as part of the
International Space University (ISU) Space Studies Program 2013 in Strasbourg.
The challenge was tackled by 33 participants from 19 countries.

The mission statement was to offer **sustainable and affordable internet access
and environmental monitoring via a constellation of microsatellites by 2018**,
as a means to provide socio-economic benefits to the Brazilian Amazon region,
with a future capability for global expansion to other remote areas.

The team was chaired by Marco Chamon (INPE) with co-chair Walter Abrahão,
teaching associate Raycho Raychev and emerging chair Dag Evensberget.

## The challenge

Around 26 million people in the Brazilian Amazon were without reliable
internet access. Terrestrial networks struggle in a region of dense forest,
long distances and limited infrastructure, widening the digital divide with the
rest of the country.

AMBIEnT had to address several linked problems at once:

- Providing basic connectivity at a cost communities could actually sustain.
- Delivering practical services, not only bandwidth: education and healthcare.
- Giving communities tools to help detect and report illegal deforestation.
- Keeping programme costs low enough to be realistic for a microsatellite
  operator and its partners.
- Meeting international space debris mitigation guidelines.

## System concept

The architecture was split into three segments, linked through a ground
gateway to the wider internet.

### Space segment

- A constellation of 100 kg microsatellites in a 600 km equatorial orbit
  (0° inclination), giving continuous coverage over the target region.
- Based on the SSTL-100 platform, a bus designed for a seven-year lifetime and
  with a strong flight heritage.
- X-Band communications using helical antennas.
- Compact spacecraft of roughly 0.4 × 0.4 × 0.7 m, suitable for low-cost
  piggyback launches.

### Ground segment

- Two internet gateway control stations (West and East) with 3 m receiving
  antennas and signal distribution systems.
- A network of ground stations with helical antennas acting as community hubs.
- Sensor drones attached to ground stations, relaying environmental data to
  the AMBIEnT processing centre.

### User segment

- Municipalities hosting tele-education centres in schools and libraries.
- Public internet terminals in each participating municipality.
- Telemedicine terminals with health-sensor packages linking patients to
  doctors elsewhere in Brazil.
- Environmental sensors, smartphones and computers connecting to local hubs.

> **Note on figures:** the October 2013 poster describes a design of 14
> satellites, 1,161 ground stations and 129 municipalities at an estimated
> programme cost of $263M. The February 2014 presentation describes an updated
> baseline of 12 satellites, 110 ground stations, about 48,000 households
> served, 6–7 years of operational time and a total cost of about $260M over
> 10 years. The design evolved between the two presentations.

## Services

### Tele-education

Partnerships with municipalities would establish tele-education centres in
schools and libraries, supported by technical education so that local people
could operate and maintain the equipment.

### Telemedicine

Two options were considered:

- **Partnership model:** AMBIEnT supplies connectivity and existing telehealth
  services provide the medical service.
- **Own platform:** a laptop with text and voice capabilities, a simple user
  interface and a health-sensor package.

### Environmental monitoring

Fixed and mobile ground sensing, together with smartphone apps and weather
monitoring, would support timely alerts of illegal deforestation. The aim was
to empower communities to act as monitors of their own forests.

## Sustainability and business model

AMBIEnT was designed to be provided at no cost to end users. Funding was
proposed through financial support from carbon-offsetting collaboration, with
the potential for microfinance and new local markets to help sustain the
programme and reduce the digital divide.

**Implementation milestones:**

- 2015: start of production
- 2016: ground station placement begins
- 2017: first of four launches, with on-orbit testing
- 2018: constellation begins operations
- 2019–2024: continued training of key personnel

The system was planned to operate for at least seven years, with a goal of a
ten-year programme.

## Space debris mitigation

A deorbit analysis showed the satellites decaying from 600 km within about 18
years, satisfying the space debris mitigation guidelines of UN COPUOS (2010)
and the Inter-Agency Space Debris Coordination Committee (IADC).

## Presentations and outputs

- **6th Wernher von Braun Memorial Symposium, 7–10 October 2013:** research
  poster presented through the University of Alabama in Huntsville and the
  American Astronautical Society.
- **UN COPUOS Scientific and Technical Subcommittee, 14 February 2014:**
  presentation of the team project, delivered by Anita Rinner (ISU SSP 2013
  alumna).
- **Project video:** produced by Dawoon Jung.

## What made AMBIEnT distinctive

- An innovative low-cost solution built around microsatellites.
- A packaged offer combining telemedicine, education terminals and
  mitigation of illegal logging, rather than connectivity alone.
- A clear value proposition for communities, with the possibility of
  microfinance.
- Sustainability built into both the business model and the orbital design.

## Limitations and status

AMBIEnT was a conceptual design study and educational exercise, not a funded
flight programme. Its figures, schedules and costs were planning estimates
produced within the constraints of the ISU Space Studies Program, and would
require detailed engineering, regulatory, spectrum and financing work before
any implementation.
