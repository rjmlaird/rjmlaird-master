## Overview

**Rosetta Strato** was a digital campaign built around the story of ESA's
**Rosetta mission** and the **Philae lander**.

The project used design, digital storytelling and educational communication to
bring the mission to a wider audience. It connected the scientific and
engineering story of Rosetta and Philae with a public-facing campaign designed
to generate interest, communicate the significance of the mission and support a
charitable initiative.

The project sits at the intersection of **space communication, digital design,
science education and campaign storytelling**.

## The mission story

ESA's Rosetta mission became one of the defining planetary-science missions of
its era, travelling to and studying a comet and deploying the Philae lander to
its surface.

The mission provided an unusually compelling story for public engagement:
a spacecraft travelling across the Solar System, rendezvousing with a comet and
attempting a controlled landing on a small, active planetary body.

Rosetta and Philae therefore provided both a scientific subject and a powerful
communications narrative.

The Rosetta Strato campaign built on this story to create a more accessible
public-facing experience.

## Campaign purpose

The campaign was intended to translate a complex space mission into an
engaging digital story.

Rather than presenting the mission only through technical information, the
project focused on the human-readable narrative surrounding Rosetta and
Philae.

The campaign combined:

- Space-science storytelling
- Digital design
- Visual communication
- Educational engagement
- Public outreach
- Campaign promotion
- Charity fundraising

This made the project both a communications exercise and an example of using
space exploration as a vehicle for public engagement.

## Communication approach

The campaign centred on the idea that the story of a space mission can be made
accessible without removing the scientific context.

Rosetta and Philae offered a particularly strong narrative structure because
the mission contained clear stages and moments of public interest: the journey
through space, arrival at the comet, deployment of Philae and the subsequent
scientific and engineering challenges.

The digital campaign therefore used the mission itself as the organising
narrative.

The emphasis was on making the subject visually engaging and understandable
for audiences who might not otherwise encounter detailed planetary-science
material.

## Design

Design was central to the project.

The visual treatment was intended to create an immediate connection with the
Rosetta and Philae story while providing a clear route through the campaign
content.

The project demonstrates how visual design can support science communication
by providing a framework through which complex subjects can be explored.

The campaign imagery and associated digital material provided a recognisable
identity for the project while retaining the connection to the underlying
mission.

## Digital campaign

The project was developed as a digital campaign rather than simply as a static
information page.

The campaign connected visual material, mission storytelling and external
resources to provide audiences with multiple ways of engaging with the
Rosetta/Philae story.

The campaign included a video component and was associated with a charitable
campaign hosted through Indiegogo.

## Educational engagement

The Rosetta and Philae story naturally lends itself to education because it
connects fundamental scientific concepts with a real space mission.

The campaign provided an opportunity to communicate subjects including:

- Cometary science
- Spacecraft exploration
- Planetary science
- Space engineering
- Mission operations
- Scientific discovery
- The challenges of operating spacecraft far from Earth

The project therefore demonstrates how a contemporary space mission can be
used as the basis for informal science education and public engagement.

## Role of storytelling

One of the central lessons from the project is the value of narrative in
science communication.

A space mission contains an enormous amount of technical information, but
audiences do not necessarily engage with technical information simply because
it is available.

Rosetta and Philae already possessed a compelling narrative. The role of the
campaign was to make that narrative accessible through design and digital
communication.

This approach helps bridge the gap between:

**scientific achievement → understandable story → public engagement**

It demonstrates how creative communication can extend the reach of space
science beyond specialist audiences.

## Lessons learned

The Rosetta Strato campaign illustrates several principles relevant to space
and science communication:

### 1. Start with the story

Major scientific projects often contain compelling narratives that can provide
the structure for public communication.

### 2. Use design to reduce barriers

Good visual communication can make technically complex subjects easier to
approach without requiring audiences to understand the underlying science in
advance.

### 3. Connect communication to action

A campaign can move beyond awareness by giving audiences a meaningful way to
participate, such as educational engagement or charitable support.

### 4. Treat space as a human story

Space exploration is ultimately carried out by people, organisations and
communities. Communicating those stories can make technically sophisticated
missions much more relatable.

## Project significance

Rosetta Strato is an example of how **space exploration, digital design and
science communication can be brought together within a single campaign**.

The project used one of the most engaging stories in modern planetary
exploration — Rosetta's journey to a comet and the deployment of Philae — as a
foundation for public-facing communication.

Its lasting value lies not only in the campaign itself, but in demonstrating
the potential of creative communication to make ambitious scientific and
engineering projects accessible to broader audiences.
