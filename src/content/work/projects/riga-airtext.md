## Overview

**Riga airTEXT** is a free public service that provides air-quality forecasts
for Riga, Latvia. It was designed to help residents and visitors understand
local conditions and prepare for short periods of poor air quality.

The service brings together Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (**CAMS**)
information, satellite observations and high-resolution urban modelling. It
delivers forecasts up to three days ahead through its website, mobile
application, social channels and free SMS or email alerts. 

I contributed editorial and science-communication work through Design & Data,
supporting a clear public story about how atmospheric data can become useful,
timely information for a city and its residents.

## The challenge

Air pollution is both technical and personal.

The underlying science involves atmospheric chemistry, emissions, satellite
observations, weather patterns, numerical forecasting and local dispersion
models. But the people who need the information may simply want to know whether
conditions will affect a journey, outdoor activity, child, respiratory illness
or allergy symptoms.

The communication challenge was therefore to translate a complex technical
system into a practical public-service question:

> What will air quality be like where I am, and what should I know before I go
> outside?

The project needed to make the scientific infrastructure credible without
overwhelming the audience with scientific jargon.

## Riga airTEXT

Riga airTEXT launched at Riga City Hall on 27 February 2018. It provides
regularly updated, three-day forecasts for air quality, ultraviolet radiation,
pollen concentrations and air temperature. 

The service was developed by UK and Latvian scientists from Cambridge
Environmental Research Consultants (**CERC**) and Estonian, Latvian &
Lithuanian Environment (**ELLE**), with financial support from CAMS. Riga City
Council and Latvia’s environmental authorities worked with the project team to
ensure the service addressed local public needs. 

## Forecast information

Riga airTEXT presents local forecast information through multiple channels:

- A public website.
- A mobile application.
- Free SMS alerts.
- Email alerts.
- Facebook and Twitter updates.
- Local air-quality maps and bulletins.

The service uses an air-quality index based on the pollutant expected to have
the greatest health impact on a given day and location. Its public index covers
nitrogen dioxide (\\(\\mathrm{NO\_2}\\)), particulate matter
(\\(\\mathrm{PM\_{10}}\\) and \\(\\mathrm{PM\_{2.5}}\\)) and ozone
(\\(\\mathrm{O\_3}\\)). 

## Technical foundation

The forecasting system combines several complementary sources of information:

Satellite observations
+ CAMS regional air-quality forecasts
+ Meteorological forecasts
+ High-resolution urban dispersion modelling
= Local public air-quality forecasts
