## Overview

**Getting Vega's Launch Tower Moving** is a short European Space Agency
documentary about the mobile launch tower used to prepare Vega rockets at
Europe’s Spaceport in French Guiana.

The film explores the engineering required to move the large service structure
away from the launch vehicle before liftoff. Although the operation appears
straightforward from a distance, it depends on careful structural design,
materials engineering, mechanical systems and launch-site operations.

I contributed script development for the film through Design & Data, helping
translate specialised engineering work into a clear story for a general
audience.

## The subject

Vega is Europe’s launcher for small satellite payloads. At the launch pad, the
rocket is assembled and checked inside a mobile building that protects
personnel, equipment and the launch vehicle during preparation.

Before launch, the entire structure moves away from the rocket, leaving the
vehicle exposed and ready for liftoff. ESA describes the mobile building as
approximately 50 metres tall and weighing more than 1,000 tonnes. [web:209]

The film focuses on the practical question behind this operation:

> How do you safely move a 1,000-tonne launch tower while maintaining the
> precision, reliability and safety required for a space launch campaign?

## Communications challenge

Launch infrastructure is essential to every mission but is often less visible
than the rocket itself.

The editorial challenge was to make a highly technical process understandable
without reducing it to spectacle. The script needed to connect:

- The familiar visual moment of a launch tower rolling away.
- The scale and mass of the mobile service structure.
- The materials and engineering decisions required for reliable movement.
- The safety requirements of launch operations.
- Vega’s role in Europe’s space transportation system.

The resulting narrative used the movement of the tower as an entry point into
the wider systems engineering that makes a launch possible.

## My role

I worked as **Scriptwriter** for the documentary.

My contribution included:

- Developing the documentary narrative and script structure.
- Translating technical source material into accessible language.
- Connecting materials science with visible launch-site engineering.
- Supporting the communication of technical interviews and visual sequences.
- Framing launch-pad infrastructure in the context of Vega operations.

The work formed part of editorial and science-communication activity delivered
through Design & Data for the European Space Agency.

## Editorial approach

The script was built around a simple physical contrast:

A large, heavy structure must move with controlled precision
to make spaceflight possible.
